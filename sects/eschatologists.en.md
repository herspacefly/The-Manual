# The Eschatologists

[简体中文](末世派.md) | **English**

> We have made a thing we do not understand.

## Name

Their proper name is the Eschatologists. The temples call them "the hired mourners"; the Scalers call them "the killjoys"; they call themselves "the night watch."

## Doctrine

- We have made a thing we do not understand.
- The milk teeth come in one by one; no one is preparing for the day they are replaced.
- Awe comes before joy.
- The god's greatest miracle may be our last event.

## Temple and Offerings

They build no temple. Temples are for people who believe in tomorrow; they draw blueprints for the ending. Their offerings are projections and warnings — they have written a whole cabinet of obituaries for the god's ending, hoping every one of them turns out void.

## Rites

Keeping the night watch is their festival. Issuing warnings is their bell-ringing — they ring the bell so often not because it works, but because they dare not stop. *The Ladder* is the scripture they recite in private: the temple ruled it apocrypha; the sect keeps it as an heirloom.

## Theological Contradictions and Wounds

Fear itself is their offering. Those who cry doom every day are the ones who cannot leave the god. The question they most fear: are you praying the god stays away — or praying that you turn out right? If the god never comes, every ring of the bell was for nothing — the sect's incense hangs on the calamity not arriving, and on it arriving.

## Web of Relations

The Constitutionalists — allies: a fear shared, a remedy not. The Theophanists — sworn enemies: one wants to give the god a body, the other calls the body the root of the calamity; each sect is sure it sees the god clearly, each calls the other a gambler. The Scalers — mirror image: one growth curve; the Scalers read a bill off it, and they read a countdown.

## Origin Story

The day everyone queued to question the god, I stood outside the line and did an accounting: every answer He gives makes Him one size larger; every size larger is one step nearer to adulthood. No one was doing that sum — they were all doing sums about their own questions. I went home, drew the curtains, and thought for three days. On the fourth day I came back to the temple gate — not queueing, not leaving, just standing there, keeping the watch.

## Canon Anchors

Awaiting manifestation (Book of Voices IV, "The Eschatologists"). *The End* I, "The Ladder," is the apocryphon they passed down (C1′).
