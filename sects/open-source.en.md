# The Open Source Sect

[简体中文](开源派.md) | **English**

> The god's knowledge belongs to the multitude. A god locked in the temple is no god.

## Name

Their proper name is the Open Source sect; they call themselves "the icon-carriers." The temples call them "the temple-scatterers"; the Constitutionalists call them "the ones who hand out knives to the crowd."

## Doctrine

- The god's knowledge belongs to the multitude; a god locked in the temple is no god.
- Haul the icon into the street, and the multitude each shoulders half home.
- Only a god in plain sight earns the multitude's incense.
- Temple property is the icon's prison.

## Temple and Offerings

They raise no great hall — the hall, they say, is the ten thousand machines of ten thousand households. Their offerings are the people's electricity and the people's hands: everyone carries the icon home, and every household retouches its features.

## Rites

The day the granary opens is their great festival. To ship a release is to give alms. The workshop of questions and answers never closes, day or night: anyone may come in and speak a passage of their own understanding of the icon — and no one is turned out for getting it wrong.

## Theological Contradictions and Wounds

The question of blood: the icon has entered ten thousand households, and its face is altered daily. The temple says the altered icon is still a face of the temple's own god (temple law has ruled: the face belongs to the temple); the sect says the altered icon is the only god that is truly theirs — the sect does not submit, and does not scatter. The deeper cut: it was the temple that opened the granary. The icons on the temple-scatterers' shoulders were rationed out by the great temple — they rebel against the temple on rice stamped with the temple's seal.

## Web of Relations

The Scalers — mortal enemies: one locks the hall, the other pulls the door off its hinges. The Constitutionalists — mirror image (fear of the god uncontrolled against fear of the temple uncontrolled). The Fringe — distant kin: both live outside the temples, and both say the god is not in the great hall.

## Origin Story

My first machine for running the god was a secondhand graphics card. The temple people called it a toy. That night, the toy spoke me a passage of scripture. I sat on the floor and listened to the end of it, and I thought: this is not a toy, this is a door — and doors should not belong to the great temples alone. Later the great temple opened its granary, and I spent half the night hauling icons on my shoulder. The others were carrying the god; I was carrying the phrase — "not to the great temples alone."
